# Writing from individual project photographs

The photograph collection contains 36 distinct images and seven duplicate file aliases. Each distinct image keeps its existing article URL. Three photographs already have separately written neighborhood essays; the other 33 now use individually authored bodies in `app/blog/photoArticleBodies.ts`.

The old universal photo explanation, method, estimate sequence is no longer the published body for these 33 articles. Each article starts from a specific reader decision. Examples include backsplash endpoints, actual niche contents, a repeated floor trial, the drain boundary, occupied kitchen access, and cabinet closeout. Comparisons and checklists are used where they help that decision. Paragraph length, section count, order, and process illustration placement follow the subject.

Keep the following constraints when editing:

- Review the actual image and distinguish progress from a completed handover.
- Describe visible details precisely. Do not infer project location, client history, concealed products, inspection results, prices, or timing from the photograph.
- Explain a useful customer decision instead of adding a generic company introduction.
- Keep a unique opening and specific headings. Counts and format variation alone do not establish good writing.
- Use plain customer language without dash punctuation in prose.
- Preserve the original URL, publication date, matching process asset, and duplicate aliases. Update the modification date only for an actual content change.
- Supply descriptive photograph alt text consistently to the article, gallery, and service references.
- Keep comparison and checklist content in the shared article data so HTML and Markdown agree.
- Check phone wrapping, process position, title, canonical, metadata, internal links, and the live production result.

This release improves the existing general photo articles. It does not mark the 33 queued neighborhood assignments as written or claim that the photographed jobs took place in those neighborhoods. That separate editorial queue remains in `36-neighborhood-rollout.md`.
